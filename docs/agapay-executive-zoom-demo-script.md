# AGAPAY executive Zoom demo

Prepared September 23, 2026 for a conversation with MotorClick and executives from John Daniel Associates.

Confirmed objectives: explore a potential partnership and help the individuals bring AGAPAY to their own churches. Working assumption: a joint meeting; 30 minutes total, with 20 minutes of presentation and 10 minutes of discussion. Meeting length and the attendees' church roles are not yet confirmed.

The primary practical ask is an introduction to each interested person's priest, treasurer, or parish administrator for a focused parish demonstration. The second ask is a separate partnership working session if there is mutual interest. Keep parish purchasing decisions and commercial partnership terms distinct.

## Positioning and audience

Core message: **AGAPAY connects giving, parish life, and financial stewardship around the way an Orthodox parish operates.**

John Daniel Associates describes its work in analytics, data integration, and performance management. Emphasize consistent definitions, financial visibility, implementation, and the decisions a report supports. Do not imply an existing integration with its products or partners. Sources: [company website](https://www.johndaniel.com/) and [analytics practice](https://www.johndaniel.com/analytics/).

MotorClick's identity could not be established from the supplied domain: [MotorClick.com](https://motorclick.com) redirected to a domain-sale page during research. Do not infer an automotive use case or associate attendees with another similarly named company. Invite their perspective on adoption and business execution without assigning them an unverified specialty.

## Spoken script and screen cues

### 0:00–2:00 — Open with the problem

**Say:**

“Thank you all for making the time. I built AGAPAY around a practical problem: parish life involves giving, households, ministries, communication, and finances, and those responsibilities often land on a small group of people.

“My aim is to make that work easier to coordinate while preserving what is distinctive about Orthodox parish life.

“I'd love you to look at this through two lenses today: as people who might bring it to your own churches, and as business leaders who might help us bring it to more parishes.

“I'll show you three perspectives: a parishioner participating, staff managing the work, and leadership understanding the finances. Then we can talk about what would be useful at your church and whether there's a partnership worth exploring.”

**Ask aloud:** “At your own church, where do you see the most friction today: giving, communication, or the treasurer's work?”

Take one short response from each group. Adjust emphasis, but preserve discussion time.

**Show:** [AGAPAY homepage](https://agapay.app), briefly.

**Say:** “The focus is Orthodox parishes. That focus shapes the language, the calendar, and the workflows you're about to see.”

### 2:00–6:00 — Follow a parishioner's experience

**Show:** A pre-authenticated demonstration account in My AGAPAY. Open giving; select an available fund; show an example amount and the available recurrence controls. Stop before submitting a payment.

**Say:**

“Imagine someone wants to support the parish consistently. They should be able to find their parish, understand the purpose of their gift, and choose how they want to give.

“Here is the giving experience. I'll use a $100 example and show the available fund and frequency options. This is a demonstration; I'm not submitting a charge.”

Pause so the audience can see the controls. If populated and verified in rehearsal, open the demonstration donor's history.

“Think about the people at your own church. Would they be able to do this confidently, with little explanation? Clear giving and useful records are the starting point.”

**Presenter note:** The $100 form example and the existing accounting books are separate illustrations. Do not suggest that an unsubmitted payment appears downstream in the ledger.

### 6:00–9:00 — Show parish life beyond giving

**Show:** One populated Koinonia screen, then one populated directory or ministry screen. Use fictional demonstration records only.

**Say:**

“A parishioner's relationship with the parish extends far beyond a gift. Here they can see parish communication and participate in community life.

“The Orthodox context matters. The liturgical calendar gives the experience a rhythm people already recognize. On the staff side, households and ministries provide a practical structure for organizing parish work.

“Think about where your church currently puts announcements, ministry information, and household details. This is the experience you could bring back to the people who manage that work.

“The product hypothesis is that bringing these recurring needs together makes AGAPAY useful throughout the week. Adoption and repeat use are things we should measure in a pilot.”

**Transition:** “Now let's move from participation to the treasurer's view.”

### 9:00–15:00 — Give executives a reason to trust the depth

**Show:** [St. Fiacre demonstration dashboard](https://agapay.app/parish/dashboard?parish=st-fiacre), Accounting, Treasurer view. Confirm the tenant label is **St Fiacre (Demo)**.

**Say:**

“These are synthetic parish books, not a customer's accounts or proof of live payment settlement. They let us look at the work a treasurer needs to perform.”

**Overview and funds — about 90 seconds.**

“Start with two different questions: how much cash is on hand, and what purposes are the resources assigned to? The fund view helps preserve that distinction. A building fund is a purpose for resources; it isn't automatically another bank account.”

Point to cash and two fund balances. The September 16 fixture recorded cash of **$37,587**, general operating net assets of **$31,871.75**, and a building fund of **$1,350**. Speak exact amounts only after confirming the current screen.

**Payables — about 60 seconds.**

“Here are the bills and their status. This supports the next operational question: what obligations do we need to plan for?”

Open one existing bill. The prepared fixture included utilities, church supplies, and roof repair, totaling **$2,023.15**. Do not save a new bill or post a payment during the standard demonstration.

**Reconciliation — about 90 seconds.**

“A report is more useful when the underlying records can be checked against a statement. This is the reconciliation workflow.”

Use the prepared **January 1–July 31, 2026** statement. If the fixture is unchanged, Review matches shows one **$27 candle deposit** suggestion. Explain the suggestion without confirming it.

“This proposed match connects an existing record with the statement item. It does not create another donation.”

**Reports and budget — about two minutes.**

Show a Balance Sheet, then Budget Variance. If showing Cash Flows, set **July 1–31, 2026** and apply report dates. The prepared July example had **$12,587** cash movement and zero reconciliation difference.

“This is the leadership view: what resources do we have, what obligations exist, and where do actual results differ from the budget?

“For the John Daniel team, this is where I'd especially value your perspective: which definitions, checks, and measures would you want established before a leadership team relied on these reports?”

Take a brief response; reserve a deeper discussion for the final ten minutes.

### 15:00–17:00 — Explain adoption and the business model

**Show:** [Current public plans](https://agapay.app/give#pricing). Check the live page before rehearsing specific prices or inclusions.

**Say:**

“The adoption model gives a parish a way to start with giving and select broader capabilities as its needs grow. The practical implementation questions are who owns setup, what data comes over, and how we support the people using it.

“These are the current published plans. I want the commercial discussion to connect the price with the actual implementation and support required.”

**If asked about fees:** “Let's distinguish the AGAPAY subscription, any applicable platform fees, and payment processing. I'll use the published terms for the specific plan and transaction type.”

Do not generalize donation pricing to commerce, describe processing as free, or offer unverified discounts. Do not claim customer counts, revenue, retention, savings, or a market size without actual supporting figures.

### 17:00–20:00 — Bring both audiences into the decision

**Stop screen sharing. Say:**

“You've seen the parishioner's experience, the parish's operating tools, and the financial view.

“For the MotorClick team, I'd welcome your assessment of the customer journey: where does the value become clear, and where might adoption stall?

“For John Daniel, I'd welcome your assessment of the information model: what would you want to validate before turning these workflows into dependable management insight?

“I see two possible next steps.

“First, if you see a fit for your own church, would you be comfortable introducing me to your priest and whoever handles giving or the books? We could do a short demonstration around their actual needs. You don't need to make a purchasing decision for the parish today.

“Second, if you see a business fit, let's schedule a separate partnership conversation. We could explore introductions to parishes, onboarding and implementation support, or reporting expertise, depending on what each team wants to contribute. We'd start by defining the contribution and the value to a parish before discussing commercial terms.”

**Close with:** “Would a conversation with your own parish be the most useful first step, a partnership working session, or both?”

If they choose a parish introduction, identify the relevant person and offer a short forwardable overview. If they choose partnership exploration, identify a lead from each interested organization and one concrete use case for the next meeting. Do not negotiate exclusivity, referral compensation, or equity during the product walkthrough.

## Discussion prompts and answers

- **How do I bring this to my church?** “Let's start with the person responsible for the problem you want to solve. I can show your priest, treasurer, or administrator the relevant workflow and help them assess the setup required.”
- **What kind of partnership are you considering?** “Possible starting points are parish introductions, implementation support, or reporting expertise. I'd like to understand what fits your team, then scope one specific engagement before proposing terms.” These are discussion options, not existing partner programs.

- **What makes this distinctive?** “The Orthodox context shapes the workflows: parish participation, liturgical timing, designated giving, and parish operations. The demonstration is meant to show how those fit together.”
- **Does it integrate with our systems?** “Let's identify the system and the specific data flow. I can distinguish what is supported today from work we would need to scope.” Do not imply existing Qlik, IBM, Vena, or MotorClick integrations.
- **Is everything shown ready today?** “I'll distinguish the capabilities in the demonstrated environment from anything that still needs validation.” Existing code and older test results do not establish that every authenticated screen works today.
- **Can this serve other industries?** “The current product and onboarding are church-first. Other markets would need separate product and implementation work.” Dormant organization classifications are not launched verticals.
- **What traction do you have?** Supply only verified current figures. If unavailable: “I don't have a verified figure to present today. I'll follow up with the actual number and its definition.”
- **What would a pilot measure?** Propose task-completion rate, time to onboard, support requests, time to reconcile a sample statement, and whether leadership can answer its agreed financial questions. These are proposed measures, not achieved outcomes.
